Syllabus

This course introduces the fundamental ideas and algorithms of applied and numerical linear algebra. Linear algebra is a central tool in modern computing and forms the mathematical foundation of many techniques in artificial intelligence, machine learning, data science, scientific computing, and statistics. The course emphasizes not only the mathematical concepts behind matrix computations, but also the algorithms used to perform them efficiently and accurately on computers. Topics include systems of linear equations, orthogonality, matrix norms, least-squares problems, QR factorization, conditioning and numerical stability, eigenvalue problems, and the singular value decomposition (SVD). Throughout the course, computational examples and applications illustrate how these methods are used in practice for data analysis, optimization, and machine learning. By the end of the course, students will understand the mathematical principles underlying modern matrix computations, be able to analyze the reliability and efficiency of fundamental numerical algorithms, and appreciate the role of linear algebra in contemporary computational applications.
Week Lectures Topics
1 1–2 Introduction. Matrix-vector multiplication, orthogonal vectors and matrices.
2 3–5 Vector and matrix norms. Introduction to the Singular Value Decomposition.
3 6–8 Projectors, QR factorization, and Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization.
4 9–11 Householder triangularization and least squares problems.
5 12–14 Conditioning, floating-point arithmetic, and numerical stability.
6 15–18 Stable algorithms and least squares. Numerical examples and applications.
7 — Midterm Examination
8 19–22 Gaussian elimination, pivoting, LU and Cholesky factorizations.
9 23–24 Structured matrices and conditioning.
10 25–27 Eigenvalues and eigenvalue problems.
11 28–29 Reduction techniques and the QR algorithm.
12 30–31 Computing eigenvectors and the Singular Value Decomposition.
13 Selected topics Applications of the SVD to data analysis, machine learning, and scientific computing.
14 — Review and Final Examination

Homework and Evaluation

Though we will fill in some background, this course will miss some standard linear algebra results. There will be suggested problems, and suggested readings that will fill in some background if you do not have it. The suggested problems will not be checked, but will come up on the tests. There will be a midterm and final tests each worth half of your final mark.
